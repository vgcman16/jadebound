# Equipment rarity, character art, and VFX reference

Research date: 2026-10-02 UTC. Scope: extensive public Conquer Online reference research for an **original** martial-fantasy MMORPG. This document supersedes any interpretation of the earlier brief that would favor toy-like proportions or undifferentiated low-poly primitives.


## Executive findings

1. **Super is the top of the documented base equipment-quality ladder:** Normal → Refined → Unique → Elite → Super. It is distinct from item level, the +n composition bonus, socket count, gem quality, purification phase, and later progression systems. [Official equipment improvement](https://co.99.com/guide/guides/improve.shtml)
2. **Super weapon glow is explicitly documented.** The official Item FAQ says Super weapons glow and mentions effects on Super and gemmed items. It does not supply a universal color, a complete slot-by-slot effect catalogue, or proof that every Super accessory produces a body aura. [Official Item FAQ](https://co.99.com/guide/faq/items.shtml)
3. **The spectacular whole-character look is layered.** Official sources separately document high-phase Dragon Soul visuals, weapon appearance overrides, wings, costumes, and skill effects. A screenshot containing all of these cannot establish what Super rarity alone adds.
4. **The inspected official characters read as detailed stylized adults:** shaped anatomy, long articulated limbs, narrow waist, layered costume/armor, defined materials, and elegant elongated weapons. The useful target is detailed martial-fantasy illustration rendered at gameplay scale, not oversized heads on boxes and cylinders.
5. **The supplied video remains unobserved.** Its page loads, but the actual player never delivers a decoded frame in this cloud browser. No visual claim below is attributed to that clip.

## 2. Equipment-quality ladder and what “rare” means here

The base ladder is documented as five named grades. “Rarity” in casual discussion often mixes this quality with scarcity, upgrade investment, and visual prestige. The sources do not provide a reliable universal drop probability for each grade, so no odds are assigned here.

| Base quality | Documented meaning | Verified visual claim | Safe original-game translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | Baseline quality of a base item | No universal glow requirement established | Material finish, shape, and readable icon; no constant magical emissions |
| Refined | First named upgrade above Normal | No source establishes a mandatory body aura | Better finishing/detail and a clear quality label; restrained accent |
| Unique | Above Refined | No universal hue or mandatory silhouette change verified | Distinct trim/motif variation, stronger item identity |
| Elite | Below Super | No complete per-slot emission specification verified | More elaborate edge/engraving treatment; quality readable even without VFX |
| Super | Highest grade in the base ladder | Explicitly glowing weapons; some other special visual effects documented but not exhaustively specified | Highest-tier weapon-local luminous edge/core, controlled particles, and a distinct item-card treatment |

Sources: [equipment upgrade ladder](https://co.99.com/guide/guides/improve.shtml), [Super-glow FAQ](https://co.99.com/guide/faq/items.shtml).

**Do not merge quality and level.** The official armor catalogue has Super versions of low-level armor as well as high-level armor. The blade catalogue similarly lists the quality ladder within each level/base-item group. A low-level Super item is still low-level equipment, and a higher-level Normal item is not automatically Super. The tables also contain multiple plain-name stat rows; do not infer additional named grades from these without evidence. [Trojan armor catalogue](https://co.99.com/guide/items/mails.shtml), [blade catalogue](https://co.99.com/guide/items/blades.shtml)

**Unverified details:** a universal inventory text-color mapping, exact rarity drop rates, every historical level threshold for armor emissions, and each weapon family's precise Super animation. The research found stronger evidence for the ladder and the existence of weapon glow than for those finer claims. Use an original accessible color system rather than inventing “official” colors.

## 3. Mechanics and visual-signals matrix

“Effect” can mean a gameplay/stat effect or a visible particle/animation effect. The distinction is essential when reading these guides.

| Layer | What it changes in the reference | Visual signal supported by evidence | What must not be inferred | Original asset/data requirement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base item / slot / class | Item family and eligibility; armor, headgear, shield, weapon, jewelry etc. | Class-specific equipment categories and multiple level-based designs appear in the catalogues | That all classes share one armor silhouette | Mesh family, attachment points, category icon, equipped and dropped representation |
| Item level | Base attributes and equip requirements; catalogues change item names/design group with level | Catalogued visual progression is associated with base equipment tiers | Level equals quality, or every level needs a wholly new model | A small set of substantial silhouette upgrades rather than dozens of barely different meshes |
| Equipment quality | Normal through Super base-quality ladder | Super weapon glow explicitly documented | Universal giant halo on all Super slots | Separate quality field plus item-family-specific material/VFX variant |
| +n composition | Extra bonus level accumulated with items/stones | Numeric +n is independently displayed/described | +12 is a sixth quality grade; a generic aura grows automatically with every +n | Separate enhancement value and tooltip; only add visual changes if the new game's design explicitly calls for them |
| Sockets and gems | Sockets accept stat-modifying gems; gems have their own Normal/Refined/Super grades | Official gem guide mentions special effects and says only one random special effect is seen at a time when different gem types are inserted | Every socket creates a separate permanent ring; Super gem equals Super equipment | Socket data, gem icon, optional triggered effect with event/priority rules |
| HP enchantment | Additional HP; gems are consumed in enchantment | The “effect” in this guide is an HP/stat amount | Enchanted means a specific visual glow | Tooltip/stat layer; no mandatory particle node |

## 4. What can be said specifically about Super visuals

### High confidence

- The basic quality ladder ends in Super, and Super weapons are explicitly described as glowing in the Item FAQ
- A September 2008 official patch note separately restored graphics for level-120 armor and glow for Super Dragon Shields. This is direct evidence that armor level/category and Super shield state mattered to particular effects, and that historical bugs could suppress them. It is not a complete level-by-level armor rulebook. [Patch 5066](https://co.99.com/content/2008-09-19/20080919012428607.shtml)
- The old general FAQ identifies particular artifact visual thresholds for armor, shield, and some weapon families; the expanded artifact guide includes later phases. Those thresholds are historical, not a universal current limit. [Legacy artifact FAQ section](https://co.99.com/guide/faqs.shtml), [expanded artifact guide](https://co.99.com/guide/guides/artifact.shtml)

### Boundaries that matter to implementation

- **Super weapon:** justified to represent with unmistakable weapon-local luminosity, provided the design itself is original
- **Super armor/headgear:** some special effects exist, but no exhaustive universal rule was verified. Avoid inventing a promise that every piece or every level emits the same plume
- **Super shield:** specific documented example exists; use a distinct shield-face/rim treatment rather than applying sword particles unchanged
- **Super ring/necklace/bracelet/bag/boots:** the item quality is real, but sources inspected do not prove an individually visible body emitter on every such slot. Their first visual identity should be an excellent icon/inspect presentation
- **Full-set aura:** no general “equip all Super to get this exact aura” rule was established. The reference game's full appearance can include many other systems
- **Color and shape:** no universal “Super means purple/gold/red glow” rule was verified. The inspected artifact images have warm weapon light and green or cyan body effects, but the images do not isolate rarity

The correct implementation abstraction is therefore **quality-aware equipment visuals plus independently sourced cosmetic/skill effects**, not “if any item is Super, turn on one character-wide particle ring.”

### Proportions: precise enough to guide, honest about uncertainty

The elevated camera foreshortens standing height, and ornate headpieces/shoulders distort simple head-count measurement. Therefore the images do **not** establish an exact anatomical ratio. As an original modeling target, start with adult heroic anatomy around 7–7.5 heads in the unposed model, a visible pelvis/waist, natural elbow/knee placement, narrower wrists/ankles, and a stance with planted feet. Judge the result at the actual game camera and render size. This number is an art proposal, not a measurement of Conquer's models.

The reference can be stylized without being toy-like. The decisive characteristics are shaped forms, shading continuity, costume layering, fine edge hierarchy, and believable posing. Merely adding polygons, particles, a larger sword, or more props will not repair a blocky mannequin.

### Material and VFX reading

- Armor has bright edge accents and dark recessed regions, preserving depth even when small on screen
- Cloth panels use hue/value bands and patterned borders; they are not just solid cones
- Weapon effects often retain a sharp inner core, colored medium glow, and softer outer particles
- The strongest permanent effect sits around the equipped object or behind the figure; hands, face, and feet need not all be drowned in bloom
- Ground remains textured and relatively quiet; small vegetation and contact shadows anchor characters
- These are stills. Particle motion, exact emission rate, animation timing, and the cause of every streak cannot be deduced from them

One public image has been saved for local pixel review at `reference-only/official-2010-trojan-artifact.jpg`. Its adjacent README records provenance and prohibits reuse as shipped game art. No source models, textures, sounds, client files, or private-server code were acquired.

## 6. Original asset and VFX requirements by category

This is a production checklist for the new game. It specifies original assets, not recreations of the pictured equipment.

| Category | Geometry/material requirements | Animation / VFX anchors | Quality progression without confusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paired blades | Two distinct slender silhouettes, visible guard/grip, edge bevel, blade material separated from wrap and metal fittings | Independent hand sockets; tip/base trail anchors; short slash arc; contact spark | Keep the base weapon readable. Highest quality adds a tight edge/core and intermittent sparks, not a bigger invisible hitbox |
| Polearm / spear | Clear long shaft, shaped head, visible wrapping and ferrule; avoid a plain thin stick with a triangle | Two-hand poses; tip thrust path, shaft sweep trail, contact point | Upgrade the blade/guard/trim silhouette; optional high-grade energy follows the head/edge |
| Bow | Curved limbs, negative space, central grip, string/readable release direction | Draw hand, arrow nock, projectile spawn; brief release flash | Luminous limb accent and arrow motif stay subordinate to aim and projectile readability |
| Shield | Distinct rim/face/boss, thickness and bevel, held rather than glued to torso | Forearm socket; block-contact point; short directional deflection | High-grade face/rim treatment; shield-specific pulse on block, no continuous generic fireball |
| Heavy one-hand weapon | Head/haft balance, readable weight, detailed striking face | Windup/recovery; broad short impact burst rather than sword line | Warm or cool core in chosen inset areas; preserve physical volume |
| Caster implement | Elegant silhouette and distinct focal ornament; robe/weapon color separation | Casting hand, ornament/tip, spell launch, channeling loop | Idle quality effect is subtle; spell telegraph and cast burst remain separately controlled |
| Body armor | Adult rig; fitted chest/waist; layered pauldrons, bracers, skirt/tassets, greaves; cloth/metal/leather masks | Torso, shoulder/back aura sockets, cloth attachment; consistent contact shadows | Silhouette and trim progression first. Any high-grade aura is designed explicitly, not inferred as a universal Conquer rule |
| Robe / light outfit | Layered overlapping panels, shaped sleeves, contrasting hems, believable folds | Hip/leg motion, robe swing; avoid rigid full-length cone | Decorative pattern and materials distinguish tier; optional back accent leaves face/legs visible |
| Headgear | Small underlying head, distinct hair/helmet/crest; face remains readable | Head socket without scaling the skull for ornament | Local crest glint or material treatment; no body halo required |
| Boots and jewelry | Appropriate proportions; strong inventory/inspect icons; small silhouettes as practical | Footstep/dust is movement VFX, not automatically equipment rarity | Quality label/material/icon clarity; do not spawn a large emitter for every small slot |
| Garment / cosmetic | Separate appearance asset, compatible with character skeleton; intentional visibility precedence | Preserve animation proportions and hand/foot sockets | Cosmetic rarity can have its own naming; do not overwrite combat-quality data |

### Required separation in the equipment presentation model

Track base item, equipped slot, item level, quality, enhancement, sockets/gems, purification appearance, cosmetic override, and active combat states independently. A visual resolver should state which layer controls the mesh, material, idle effect, trail, and temporary effect. Unequipping, switching weapon, dying, respawning, changing costume, and loading a save must remove stale effects.

Recommended effect channels:

1. **Item idle:** small low-frequency glints/energy, anchored to gear
2. **Weapon motion:** trails generated by actual animated endpoints during attacks
3. **Hit:** short confirmed contact burst, damage/impact tied to the same gameplay event
4. **Body prestige/cosmetic:** bounded plume/ribbon behind the character, optional and separate
5. **Skill telegraph/burst:** time-limited ground/air effect, explicitly hostile/friendly and spatially informative
6. **Loot signal:** item silhouette plus label/quality mark; avoid assuming a loot beam is an authentic Conquer detail

Use per-channel visibility and intensity controls. Readability beats piling one full emitter system onto every equipped item. There is no requirement to implement every historical progression axis just to support a visually rich hero.

## 8. Highest-value changes and review criteria

### Next art pass

1. Fix skeleton pivots, proportions, joint placement, pose silhouette, and continuous limb attachment before investing in effects
2. Replace simple solid-color masses with fitted, layered armor/cloth forms and controlled bright-edge/dark-recess shading
3. Design one excellent original weapon with a clear grip/guard/blade and beautiful high-quality glow at gameplay scale
4. Make the highest quality visibly different when equipped, while keeping the same class/weapon readable; retain separate aura/cosmetic and skill layers
5. Add at least one lower-quality comparison in the same camera/lighting/pose so the actual art delta is inspectable
6. Expand to the second class only after the first character looks good standing, running, jumping, attacking, and viewed against both grass and paving

### Acceptance checks

- Does the hero read as an articulated adult rather than a toy when all VFX are off?
- Are head, shoulder, waist, hand, knee, and foot relationships believable during motion?
- Are weapon grips and foot contacts convincing, with no floating joints or independent body pieces?
- Can a viewer identify weapon family and class silhouette before reading labels?
- Does high-quality gear improve the visible equipped appearance, not just change an inventory border?
- Is item-level/base-form progression distinct from quality/enhancement/cosmetic state?
- Are effects removed cleanly after unequip, death, respawn, and load?
- Can ground hazards and landing points still be read with multiple effects on screen?
- Do armor and cloth remain shaded/readable with bloom disabled or reduced?
- Is every shipped mesh, texture, sound, icon, animation, and effect original or covered by a recorded reuse license?
